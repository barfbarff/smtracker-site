---
title: "SQD: Populations"
description: "The main analysis: fractions and apparent D's from the squared displacements."
---

This is the main analysis: the fractions and apparent D's of the diffusive states, from the distribution of squared displacements. **Compute** opens the settings.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Force same D across** | **Replicates** | What one D is forced across (choices below). |
| **Weighting** | **Step** | **Step** counts every displacement once. **Track** counts every track once, giving the share of molecules; the slow fraction is about 2.5 points lower, because long tracks are slow. |
| **Uncertainty** | **Replicates** | What the error bars are over (choices below). |
| **ECDF** | **Fast** | Evaluates the fit at 2000 points; fractions differ by under 0.001 points from **Full**. **Custom** sets the number of points. |
| **Optimiser** | **Fast** | **Fast** uses exact derivatives and is about twofold quicker at 3 populations; **Standard** estimates them numerically and gives the same numbers. |
| **Time lag** | 1 | Frames between the displacements (1 to 4). At lag 2 to 4, a diffusing state keeps its D while a population of localization error alone reads 2 to 4 times lower, which tests whether a slow population is a state or the floor. **1–4** fits all four lags at once. |
| **Localisation error** | **Not modelled** | **Per spot** gives every jump its own error (below). |
| **Models** | **Best (BIC)** | The number of populations, chosen per condition on the **Fits** page (below). |
| **Overlay** | **Off** | **Both** draws two fits side by side: **Same D across replicates** and **Same D across the whole data set**. It needs every row on one count. |

**Force same D across.**

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Replicates** | One D per condition, fitted on all its replicates together, with fractions per replicate. A population under 5 % is not kept (fit one fewer): its D is poorly determined and tests on it become too confident. |
| **Conditions** | Offered only with a folder design. One D and one count per value of the design's factor. Force D across the factor that is the molecule, never across different proteins. Named under the chart where a condition's own D and its group's differ by 1.5 times or more. |
| **Whole data set** | One D for every condition. |
| **Don't force anywhere** | Every replicate of every condition fitted on its own, D included. |

On the **Fits** page each condition row has its own **Force same D across** (**Replicates** or **Don't force anywhere**), and the **Every condition** line sets every row. Under **Conditions** and **Whole data set** the conditions share one fit, so the models are chosen on the **Every condition** line only. A significance bracket between two conditions fitted differently is refused, and a **Check the fit** line under the chart names any population under 5 % or two populations within a factor of 2 in D.

**Uncertainty.**

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **None** | Pools every replicate into one fit, with no spread. |
| **Replicates** | Fits each replicate; each bubble is the mean ± SD over replicates. |
| **Movies**, **Cells** | For a single session only. The pooled fit with a 95 % interval from resampling its movies or cells (bootstrap). The movie interval is the wider, safer one. |
| **Chunks** | For data imaged without replicates. Each condition's movies are dealt at random into chunks (5, or 2 to 20 in **Chunks per condition**) fitted as pseudo-replicates, so Welch or the equivalence test can compare conditions. They measure precision "within one culture", not where another culture would come out. |

**Movies**, **Cells** and **Chunks** are withheld from a condition imaged in two or more replicates, which is drawn as its pooled fit with no spread and a caption saying why.

**Models.** One pill per model on each condition's row of the **Fits** page: **Best (BIC)** (the number of states by BIC, 1 to 3, or 1 to 4 with **Best (BIC) also tries 4 populations** in Options), a count from 1 to 4, or **saSPT** (the modes saSPT resolves). BIC recommends; the number of states is a biological decision. Light one pill for a single fit. Light two or more on any row to fit every condition at each lit model and draw them side by side in shades of the condition's color, which is how a state count is judged. Brackets compare one model across conditions, never two models of one condition.

**Per spot.** Normally a bound molecule's jumps are read through one exponential, so a less precisely placed molecule looks faster and part of the bound population can appear as a middle one. **Per spot** gives each jump its own localization error from the precision the tracker reported (u-track always; TrackMate when **Fit each spot's precision** was on), through a calibration measured on immobile spots, and fits by maximum likelihood. It is not available for the 1–4 lags. The calibration is chosen in the project menu under **Per-spot calibration…**: **Built-in**, **This project's file** (`precision_calibration.json` in the conditions' folders or the folder above) or one from your **library**. To make one, choose **Make one from a stationary measurement…**, point it at movies of fixed cells or of protein stuck to glass (a separate measurement, tracked at your data's settings), press **Measure**, then **Add to library**. A condition without per-spot precision (a simulation's true positions, or another tool's track table) is fitted without the error model in the same Run, and the caption says so.

**Simulated true positions.** A simulation's bound steps have length exactly 0. They form their own population (D = 0, its fraction the share of zero jumps) and the other populations are fitted to the moving jumps. The count you choose is the total, so 3 reads bound, intermediate and free; one population is refused. The bubble sits at a tick labeled **0**, and a line under the chart gives the number of zero jumps. Forcing one D across such a condition and a measured one gives a warning, because the measured condition's slowest population is then empty by construction.

**Reading the bubbles.** Height is the apparent D (log scale) and area is the fraction. Click a bubble for its replicates; where you paired replicates, the table has a **Pair** column that is a label only. The ± is the spread between replicates, not a confidence interval. The slow D sits at or below the localization floor and the fast D near the detection ceiling, so read both as bounds and base conclusions on the fractions. The line under each column gives its sample ("3 replicates · 1,271 tracks"); under **Movies**, **Cells** or **Chunks** it names those, and where no spread is drawn it reads "3 replicates pooled". In the Export dialog, clear **n** (under Show) to leave it out.

**Fraction × D** (the switch beside the card's title) draws each population of each condition as a point, its fraction across and D up (log scale), colored by condition with a marker per population. A bar shows only where the Run has a spread; under **Replicates** D is shared, so it has no vertical bar. The **eye** beside a condition's name hides it, from the export too.

**Axes** (the ruler button) sets where an axis starts and ends; an empty field is automatic and **Auto** clears the range. A range that cannot be drawn (a log axis from 0, or "from" not below "to") is refused with the reason.

**Copying a table.** The copy button on the SQD card copies the data table at full precision, and a right-click on any table offers **Copy table**. Paste into Excel with Ctrl+V; a spread becomes its own column. The SQD table ends with one column per replicate, the values each bubble is the mean of.

**Metadata.** After a Run, **Metadata** shows the SQD settings and one line per replicate with its fractions (slowest first). **Copy** takes it as text.
