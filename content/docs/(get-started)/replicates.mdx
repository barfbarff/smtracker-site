---
title: How replicates are counted
description: Check that SMTracker groups your movies into the right replicates, the independent units behind every error bar, and regroup them when it doesn't.
---

A *replicate* is an independent measurement of a condition, usually a separate culture imaged on its own day. By default, the analysis computes its error bars and significance tests over replicates, not over movies or tracks, because movies from the same culture aren't independent of each other. That's why it matters that SMTracker groups your movies into the right replicates.

There are three arrangements it recognizes on its own. You don't need to choose: the analysis reads the arrangement from your folders and series numbers, and **Confirm project** shows the replicates it found before anything is computed.

These rules belong to the analysis. The preparation and outlines windows see each folder that holds movies as a condition of its own.

## 1. From the series numbers (the default)

If all of a condition's movies sit in one folder, the analysis groups them by *series number*: the first number in the movie's name, such as 1 in `series001`. Each block of ten is one replicate: series 001 to 010 is the first, 011 to 020 the second, and so on.

<ReplicateBlocksDiagram />

Replicates are numbered in the order the blocks occur, and gaps are fine. If you imaged series 001 to 008 and then 021 to 030, you get two replicates.

### When your series are numbered differently

If you didn't image ten movies per replicate, regroup them when you open the project. On **Confirm project**, click the **Groups** view. On the **Analysis groups** board, click <Ui variant="outline">Regroup…</Ui> to set groups of any number of series at once, with a preview, or drag single series between replicates. See [Opening a project](/docs/analysis/opening-a-project).

When you open the workspace, SMTracker saves each condition's grouping as `day_map.json` in its folder, and uses it from then on. So you only regroup once per condition. Movies you add later are placed automatically, and SMTracker asks you to confirm.

## 2. Replicates as sub-folders

You can also keep each replicate in a folder of its own, inside the condition's folder. The analysis opens them as one condition, with each folder one replicate.

<Files>
  <Folder name="CitZ-mNG">
    <Folder name="GM" note="one condition">
      <Folder name="GM1" note="replicate 1: series001, series002, …" />
      <Folder name="GM2" note="replicate 2: series001, series002, …" />
      <Folder name="GM3" note="replicate 3: series001, series002, …" />
    </Folder>
  </Folder>
</Files>

Here every replicate starts its series again at `series001`. The replicates are ordered by their numbers, and the movies are listed as `series001 (GM1)`, `series001 (GM2)`, and so on, so you can still tell them apart.

For this to work, the folders must be named as replicates:

- a number, after the condition's name if you like: `1`, `GM1`, `M2c`, `day 2`, `replicate 2`; or
- the full date they were imaged, which orders them by date: `10.03.2025`, `2025-03-10`.

<Callout type="check">If even one folder isn't named as a replicate (say `GM/maltose` beside `GM/GM1`), the analysis can't tell that they belong together, and opens them as separate conditions. Rename the odd one out.</Callout>

If you open the folder above the condition (`CitZ-mNG` here), the movies sit two levels down, so the analysis first asks **What do the folders mean?**. That's expected: mark the level as the replicate there.

## 3. Replicates in numbered folders

A third arrangement: each replicate has its own folder under one shared name, but the series numbers continue across the folders instead of starting again.

<Files>
  <Folder name="LB" note="one condition">
    <Folder name="LB-1" note="replicate 1: series001–010" />
    <Folder name="LB-2" note="replicate 2: series011–020" />
    <Folder name="LB-3" note="replicate 3: series021–030" />
  </Folder>
</Files>

This opens as one condition, as if all the movies sat in one folder, with each folder one replicate. The folder decides, not the series number: `LB-1` is replicate 1, whatever its series numbers say. Longer names work too, such as `GapA-LB-rif-1`, `-2`, `-3`.

- Extra folders that only wrap the replicates are fine, for example `Glu Exhaustion/20240618/…-1`, `-2`, `-3`.
- The numbers must run 1, 2, 3, and so on. If they don't (only `LB-2` and `LB-3`, or `IPTG-10` and `IPTG-100`), the folders aren't read as replicates. Opened from the folder above, `LB` is then one condition with replicates from the series numbers. Opened on its own, each folder becomes a condition of its own.

## Which arrangement the analysis uses

The series numbers decide it:

| Series numbers | Arrangement | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything in one folder | [from the series numbers](#1-from-the-series-numbers-the-default) | `LB/series001` to `series030` |
| Start again at `series001` in every folder | [replicates as sub-folders](#2-replicates-as-sub-folders) | `GM/GM1`, `GM/GM2`, `GM/GM3` |
| Continue across the folders | [replicates in numbered folders](#3-replicates-in-numbered-folders) | `LB/LB-1` (001–010), `LB-2` (011–020) |

If your replicate folders fit none of these, for example dates written as `20240101`, describe your folders on **What do the folders mean?** and mark that level as the replicate. See [Opening a project](/docs/analysis/opening-a-project).

<Callout type="note">
Replicates are never matched across conditions by their number: replicate 1 of glucose has nothing to do with replicate 1 of malate. If your replicates of different conditions really belong together (for example one culture split into two media), select **Pair replicates** when you open the project. See [Opening a project](/docs/analysis/opening-a-project).
</Callout>
